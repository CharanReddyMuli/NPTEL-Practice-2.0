Q: Which stage comes immediately after Ideation in the broad stages of product development?
A) Testing
B) Validation
C) Prototyping
D) Commercialisation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Prototyping follows ideation, turning an initial idea into a model that can be tested and improved.

Q: In MBTI personality typology, ENTJ is the:
A) Rarest personality type that makes for highly effective leaders and CEOs
B) Most commonly found personality type that makes for submissive officers
C) Unique descriptor for product designers
D) Indicator for rule-book bound CFOs and lawyers
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: ENTJ stands for Extraverted, Intuitive, Thinking, and Judging. It is often associated with leadership, although it is not the rarest MBTI type.

Q: Kerr, S.P., W.R. Kerr & T. Xu in their paper, “Personality Traits of Entrepreneurs: A review of Recent Literature”, HBS Working Paper 18-047, November 2017 held that entrepreneurial characteristics are a part of:
A) Individual training
B) National culture
C) Industrial development
D) Global economy
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The accepted answer in the provided quiz is national culture, reflecting the role of cultural context in entrepreneurship.

Q: Entrepreneurial large-scale firms may succeed in creating:
A) Self-delusion
B) Conglomerates
C) Excessive competition
D) None of the above

Q: Compared with conventional entrepreneurs, start-up founders take additional risks primarily by:
A) Experimenting with innovation and new business models
B) Experimenting with employees
C) Experimenting with public policy
D) Experimenting with customers
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Start-up founders often test innovative products, technologies, and business models under uncertainty.

Q: The success of many start-ups such as RedBus, Swiggy, Airbnb, Uber and make my trip was based on:
A) Treating demand and supply separately
B) Focussing only on demand
C) Focussing only on supply
D) Aggregating and linking demand and supply digitally
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: These platforms digitally connect customers with service providers, helping match demand with supply.

Q: The fundamental and the first step of design thinking is:
A) Designing a product or service
B) Empathising with the customer
C) Prototyping
D) Developing a market plan
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Empathy helps designers understand users' needs, experiences, and problems before developing solutions.

Q: A true design thinking and ideation exercise involves:
A) Accepting the status quo
B) Questioning the basics
C) Relying on experts
D) None of the above
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Design thinking encourages challenging assumptions and exploring new ways to solve problems.

Q: Which of the following is a serious ideation mistake?
A) Lack of boundary
B) Lack of predefined purpose
C) Bias and closed-mindedness
D) Imaginary constraints
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Bias and closed-mindedness restrict creative thinking and prevent consideration of alternative ideas.

Q: A bootcamp helps an entrepreneurial aspirant in:
A) Train oneself in basic skills
B) Create certain building blocks of a venture
C) Experience and understand team dynamics
D) All of the above
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: An entrepreneurial bootcamp develops basic skills, helps build a venture, and provides experience in teamwork.

Q: What is the correct basic sequencing of entrepreneurial product or service development?
A) Prototyping-Testing-Validation-Ideation-Commercialisation
B) Prototyping-Ideation-Testing-Validation-Commercialisation
C) Ideation-Validation-Testing-Prototyping-Commercialisation
D) Ideation-Prototyping-Testing-Validation-Commercialisation
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The basic sequence begins with ideation, followed by prototyping, testing, validation, and commercialisation.

Q: What could turn out to be the risks of prototype development?
A) Trying to make-do with off-the-shelf components
B) Chasing far-too futuristic technologies
C) Obsession with optics to the disregard of functionality
D) Any or all of the above
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Prototype development can face risks from unsuitable components, impractical technologies, and prioritising appearance over functionality.
